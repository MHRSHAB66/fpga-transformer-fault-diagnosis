# Streaming decisions, integrity, and deadline-aware reliability

Research date: 2026-11-11. Scope: genuinely new directions beyond the existing noise augmentation, k-vote, energy measurement, endurance, clean golden validation, and UART recovery lists. No board connection, probe, build, execution, or baseline modification was performed. All pilots and thresholds below are proposals, not results.

## 1. Which new direction could most strengthen the paper?

### Takeaway

The strongest fresh board-dependent direction in this scope is **vulnerability-guided integrity protection of the complete integer pipeline, including temporal decision state, under a fixed deadline**. The key research question is not whether the classifier survives bad sensors, but whether a small amount of carefully placed checking prevents a computational fault from silently becoming a believable diagnosis. A second direction is a **finite-horizon causal evidence accumulator** that improves the false-alarm/detection-delay frontier relative to ordinary k-vote. Neither idea is novel merely by name; the contribution would have to come from a specific mechanism, measured cost, and controlled comparisons on this small FPGA.

### Cited Findings

- ALBERTA profiles vulnerability and protects selected Transformer GEMMs using checksums. It supports integer and floating-point error analysis; reported coverage exceeds 99% for its injected mismatch-causing errors. Its target is GPU vision Transformers, not this INT10 Zynq pipeline. Its own analysis notes that raw fault vulnerability depends on hardware details and assumptions; a uniform software fault sampler is not a natural failure-rate model. [ALBERTA, published 2024, author paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03841v2), sections II-A and III.
- FT-Transformer 2025 combines checksums with selective protection of nonlinear attention operations and integrates checking across attention. Its evaluations concern GPU kernels and larger models. This directly rules out claiming that “checksum protection of a Transformer” is itself our invention. [FT-Transformer](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02211v1), sections 2.3–4.
- ATTNChecker is a 2025 training-oriented attention reliability method. Its experiment targets extreme floating-point errors; its reported average training overhead is 7%. That error regime differs from the bounded INT10 arithmetic here. [PNNL primary publication record](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/attnchecker-highly-optimized-fault-tolerant-attention-large-language-model-training).
- A 2025 FPGA inference fault-emulation platform inserts programmable faults into NVDLA multiplier outputs, exposes controls through AXI, and evaluates the emulator physically. It reports a 0.71% LUT / 0.31% FF increase for its variable-error implementation. These overheads belong to that architecture and cannot be transferred to this HLS design. The authors also explicitly distinguish graph-level faults from more hardware-aware injection. [Masar et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12818v1), sections I–III; [official code](https://github.com/ehw-fit/zynq-nvdla-faultinjection).
- The 2025 FPGA LLM paper distinguishes user-memory corruption from FPGA configuration-memory corruption and studies low-cost concurrent error detection. A weight-read XOR in our HLS code would be a controlled logical injection, not direct configuration-memory upset injection. [IEEE DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCASAI.2025.3552735); [author manuscript search-accessible at UPM](https://oa.upm.es/88428/3/Safe_FlightLLM-final.pdf). Full direct PDF fetch returned 403; only the author manuscript's indexed first-page passage and the publisher abstract were inspected.
- A particularly recent novelty boundary is RePAIR, published **8 September 2026**: runtime checksum checking, real configuration-memory fault emulation, partial reconfiguration, and state-preserving recovery already exist for a RISC-V plus systolic accelerator. Its publication also acknowledges limits of radiation reliability extrapolation. We should not promise first-ever FPGA fault detection/recovery or adopt its mission-rate claims. [RePAIR, ACM TRETS, DOI 10.1145/3806052](https://doi.org/10.1145/3806052). Direct open returned 403, but a later search returned the publisher's detailed abstract and sections 1–3, 6, 9; those passages were read.
- This repository's raw/vote HLS top uses generated `static const` weight arrays, shared linear modules, integer requantization, and 15 entries of fully partitioned vote history with 4-bit `seen`; the PC driver exposes the vote registers. There is no ordinary AXI-writable weight memory interface. [HLS top](../../../hls/f1_int10_seq256/vitis/transformer_top_seq256_raw1024_dsp_margin_vote.cpp), [weights](../../../hls/f1_int10_seq256/vitis/weights_int10.h), [vote state](../../../hls/f1_int10_seq256/vitis/temporal_vote.h), [driver](../../../pynq/fault_classifier.py).
- The raw core's documented post-route usage is 36,670 LUT, 76.5 BRAM tiles and 210/220 DSP, with WNS +0.167 ns; adding the deployed vote cost +241 LUT/+186 FF. These leave more room for small fabric checks than for duplicating the network. They do **not** establish that any proposed checker will route or meet timing. [Raw-core report](../../../docs/reports/onchip_preprocessing_20261109.md), [paper review, section 6](../../../docs/reports/paper_readiness_review_20261110.md).

### Inferences

#### Candidate A — integrity-aware integer inference, recommended first

**Hypothesis.** A small selective protection scheme, chosen from measured stage/bit vulnerability rather than uniformly protecting all stages, can materially reduce silent incorrect diagnoses and corrupted temporal state while retaining the 20.48 ms measured release-to-result deadline. The most interesting unknown is whether normalization/LUT/control state is more valuable to protect than the large attention MAC arrays.

**Mechanism suitable for this repository.** Keep approved model, weights, golden vectors, class map, arithmetic order and current overlay immutable. Create an isolated research artifact. First add deterministic hooks in an exact C-model at stage output / selected accumulator / selected weight read / vote-history and `seen` boundaries. A later single instrumented HLS variant can expose stage, index, bit, target-window, duration and injection-enable controls. For immutable weights, XOR the selected **read value** at a named use; do not claim the ROM was physically corrupted, and do not rebuild for every bit. Verify that injection-disabled execution equals the original reference exactly.

Separate three fault families in every table: transient single-use corruption; persistent per-window or per-read corruption; temporal-state corruption. A sign-bit flip in a 10-bit tensor, a bit in a wider accumulator, and a bit in a 32-bit host container are distinct experiments. The exact target width must be defined from the HLS types and final report. Layer-wide logical corruption and one physical shared multiplier fault also have different exposure and must not be conflated.

Potential small protectors, selected only after vulnerability profiling:

1. Duplicate the tiny final argmax and assert class/logit consistency inside PL; protect persistent vote-history entries and `seen` with parity/invariants. A PC argmax can remain an audit, but is not the final classifier.
2. For selected linear stages, checksum **pre-requantization accumulators**, not clamped outputs. Rounding/saturation breaks naive linear checksum identities after requantization. Derive the exact arithmetic width and overflow behavior or use a rigorously defined modular identity. A single sum can miss compensating errors; report coverage under the actual selected fault model rather than assert universal detection.
3. Duplicate selected nonlinear/LUT index calculations or verify exact pre-established integer invariants. A generic activation-range checker is weak here because outputs already clamp to [-511,511]. “Softmax sums to exactly one” is also not a valid assertion without proving the implementation's integer sum error bound.
4. Latch an integrity flag tied to the frame identifier. A failed result becomes an explicitly invalid diagnosis. Clearing/recovery of temporal history is a documented research-only state policy; never silently overwrite the approved continuous-history behavior.

**Minimal pilot before FPGA work.** 256 validation windows, sampled across files/classes; 8 named stage groups × 3 predeclared bit bands × 128 deterministic injections each = 3,072 C-model injection cases, plus no-fault controls. This count is a budget proposal, not a completed count. Record no effect / changed logits / changed raw class / changed stable class / invalid / timeout separately. Compare confidence distributions for wrong-but-plausible outputs, without calling confidence an error detector. Escalate the number of injections only if the pilot finds useful separation between vulnerable sites and inexpensive protection.

**Fair comparators.** Original baseline; instrumentation-only with no checker; uniform range checking; tiny classifier/state protection; vulnerability-selected protection. Use the same windows and exact same injected site/bit/time manifest for all schemes. Compare at matched LUT/FF/DSP cost or report a full coverage-cost frontier. Duplicating the entire network is a theoretical reference unless it actually fits; do not advertise it as a measured baseline. Stratified and activity-weighted fault summaries should be separate; neither is a measured radiation-rate estimate.

Define denominators explicitly: attempted injections; injection opportunities reached; actual perturbed values/state; changed logits; changed raw class; changed stable class; detected/invalid results; and silent class corruptions. Report unconditional risk over requested frames and conditional detection among actually harmful faults. Otherwise inactive hooks, masked flips, or already-wrong original predictions can make coverage look artificially good. A computational integrity flag, an input-quality flag, and a low-confidence flag describe different causes. Keep the approved four-class prediction plus separate metadata; do not introduce an undocumented fifth class.

**Proposed acceptance gate, fixed before runs.** On an untouched confirmation injection manifest: at least 80% reduction in *silent raw-class or stable-class corruptions* relative to instrumentation-only; zero false integrity flags over the original 95,404 clean inputs; injection-disabled bit-exactness on all original clean/golden inputs; <=5% additional LUT, <=2 additional DSP, timing closure on the actual part; zero normal-operation deadline misses in a predeclared 30,000-window acceptance run at the same boundary/warm-up. These are research targets, not guaranteed feasibility. Report confidence intervals and denominators; low corruption counts may make the 80% target uninformative and should trigger a larger held-out campaign, not cherry-picking.

**Critical recovery limitation.** At roughly 16 ms compute/service time and a 20.48 ms deadline there is about 4 ms slack. Repeating an entire failed inference would take roughly another 16 ms and cannot be promised within the original deadline. A credible first variant detects and flags the bad frame, restores small temporal state, and resumes on the next frame. Only replaying a measured small stage might fit current-frame slack. Do not claim “fault tolerant, zero missed deadlines” by excluding invalid frames or silently lengthening the period. Report deadline misses, valid output coverage, invalid diagnoses, recovery time and recovery-induced losses jointly.

**What absolutely needs the board.** Injection-disabled exactness; physical placement/timing/resource cost; injected outputs matching exact instrumented C-model; flag/frame association; measured latency in no-fault and injected cases; bounded state recovery on repeated campaigns; evidence that the original overlay remains reloadable. Capture instrumented and production bit/HWH, hashes, source commit, injection manifest/seed, matched raw logits/classes/flags/vote state, timestamps, timing/resource reports, and temperatures. C-model vulnerability sweeps, confidence intervals and final figures can wait until after return.

**Effort estimate, uncertain.** 2–4 working days for an exact C-model pilot and ranking; 4–8 additional days for one constrained injection/checker HLS branch and board validation, depending heavily on HLS timing. No extra equipment required. Kill the hardware expansion if checks overflow routing/resources, create false positives, or fail to improve silent-corruption risk; retain a transparent negative result and the pilot map.

#### Candidate B — finite-horizon causal evidence instead of fixed k-vote

**Hypothesis.** Four small saturating evidence states, with learned/validated finite-horizon thresholds and a defined uncertain state, can reduce detection delay at a fixed false-alarm/event-error budget relative to k=1/5/9/15. This is a temporal decision policy layered on top of an unchanged per-window FPGA Transformer, not an alternative heuristic classifier or an inference skip.

**Concrete first mechanism.** Every window still traverses preprocessing, the full Transformer and FPGA argmax. A separately named research output maintains leaky/discounted class evidence from the four integer logits, with LUT or affine calibration fitted using training/validation only. A transition is committed only when the new candidate accumulates enough evidence relative to the currently stable class. Explicitly record stable class, raw class, decision validity/age and transition start/horizon. Define bounded accumulator range, tie rules, saturation and reset/rollback semantics. A maximum horizon does not justify secretly forcing a “confident” decision; it must either emit a flagged raw prediction or remain unresolved under an explicit policy.

**Minimal pilot.** Replay saved logits in chronological within-recording order; fit thresholds on validation recordings; construct a **controlled synthetic transition benchmark** from disjoint recordings with randomized switch position and dwell lengths, including A→B→A events lasting 1,2,4,8,16 windows. Keep natural within-recording false switches separate from concatenation boundaries. Do not use labels, future windows, file IDs or switch locations as runtime reset signals. Simulation can reject weak policies before an HLS build.

**Comparators and fairness.** The current continuous k-vote including startup/transition windows; raw k=1; ordinary hysteresis; fixed-threshold leaky evidence; finite-horizon evidence. Match false switches per valid replay duration or match per-recording false-alarm budget, then compare median/p95 delay, unresolved fraction, short-event detection, and unstable re-switches. Also compare at the same maximum decision horizon. Never report only steady-state window accuracy. The production raw class remains unchanged and available.

**Proposed gate.** At a locked validation-selected false-switch budget, >=20% lower p95 detection delay than the best fixed-k baseline **without increasing unresolved-event rate**; no >0.5 percentage-point deterioration in steady-state macro-F1; zero numerical disagreement between the policy C-model and PL; <=1% additional LUT, no new DSP, original per-window deadline preserved. Use held-out recording pairs and block/file bootstrap. Abandon as a new algorithm claim if simple hysteresis is equally good; it remains an engineering option.

**Board-only capture.** Exact integer evidence trajectories (or a bounded debug subset), raw and stable classes, flags, controlled input IDs, wraparound/startup behavior, output time, post-route costs; physical tests of transitions and reset/reload semantics. Most threshold exploration and statistics can wait. Estimated 2–4 days software pilot, 3–6 days isolated HLS implementation/verification if the pilot wins; no extra instruments.

**Novelty and claim boundary.** This would be an empirically calibrated finite-horizon policy, unless we actually implement and verify the density-ratio assumptions required by an SPRT theorem. Simply summing adjacent softmax logits does not inherit SPRT error guarantees. FIRMBOUND already handles finite-horizon optimal stopping and non-i.i.d. experiments; our contribution cannot be the mere use of a finite horizon. Serial dependence, quantization, distribution shift and the tiny-state physical implementation are the problem to investigate.

### Gaps

- No measured real-hardware fault sensitivity exists for this repository yet; no natural upset rate, SEU cross section or field MTTF can be inferred from the proposed campaign.
- No new prototype exists, so all coverage, area and delay targets are feasibility gates rather than expected improvements.
- The existing ten normal test recordings give limited independent healthy exposure. False alarms per physical operating hour need a clearly timed chronological source; repeatedly replaying independent windows cannot manufacture industrial operating hours or independent statistical evidence.
- MAFAULDA recordings have static conditions. Concatenating recordings or inserting faulty snippets creates controlled synthetic transitions, not measured physical fault onset, progression or machine generalization.

## 2. Which system experiments add distinct value while the board exists?

### Takeaway

The highest-value system addition is to determine the **operating envelope under simultaneous acquisition, compute, and host activity**, including whether deadline success depends on RAM preload and isolated Linux scheduling. This is different from another quiet overnight run. It is supporting evidence on its own; a stronger contribution would add a concrete isolation or bounded-buffer mechanism and show a reproducible frontier between workload, freshness, and deadline behavior.

### Cited Findings

- The acceptance runner preloads inputs, schedules periodic releases, starts by copying one 32,768-byte input into a DMA buffer, arms S2MM, sends MM2S, waits, and finishes after reading the FPGA class. Its raw trace records release/start/send_done/done. An overrun does not move future nominal release times, so backlog appears in release-to-done response; however, there is no physical sensor producer or observable independent acquisition FIFO in this runner. [Acceptance runner](../../../tools/board/fpga_rt_acceptance.py), especially lines 129–200.
- The live runner already has prefetching and display overlap; merely proposing “use prefetch” would duplicate existing work. [Live runner](../../../tools/board/fpga_live_runner.py), `Prefetch`, `PREFETCH_DEPTH`, and the overlap path.
- MemPol 2024 presents fine-grained external bandwidth regulation, evaluated on newer multicore SoCs including ZCU102. Its R5/debug-based control mechanism does not directly map to a Zynq-7020 with Cortex-A9 cores. It supports the importance of measuring interference; it does not supply a drop-in remedy here. [MemPol](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11241-024-09422-8), abstract and conclusion.
- A 2025 Journal of Systems Architecture study finds that cache thrashing can create the worst interference for real workloads and that one workload is not universally worst for all platforms. This supports using distinct controlled stressors rather than one generic CPU benchmark. [Carletti et al., 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1383762125001596). Abstract/indexed primary passages screened; full text not deeply read.
- Older but directly relevant background: a DATE 2022 analysis measured CPU slowdowns up to 16× under shared-memory interference on newer FPGA SoCs and evaluated controlled request injection. This is motivation, not a predicted slowdown for the current board. The author-PDF search date misleadingly looked recent; publication metadata confirms 2022. [Official publication record](https://iris.unimore.it/handle/11380/1280820).

### Inferences

#### Candidate C — measured interference envelope with bounded data freshness

**Hypothesis.** Current deadline headroom may be dominated by PS scheduling and DMA memory contention rather than the arithmetic core. A deterministic data-transfer schedule and preallocated bounded queues may preserve timing across a larger workload envelope without changing the model.

**Minimal pilot, first no new bitstream.** After the active board run ends, use the existing fixed release-to-FPGA-result boundary, warm-up and temperature guard. Test idle; compute-only on the other core; bounded streaming read; bounded write/copy; cache-thrashing; and actual inference-result transport/logging. Start at modest duty levels; log the admitted workload and memory rate. All tests must include correctness matching. Report start-release jitter separately from copy/MM2S and core/S2MM stages, plus response tail/max, deadline miss count, and lag growth. A low average with rising lag is a failed operating point.

**Mechanism if a meaningful failure is found.** First compare reversible per-process placement, preallocation, paced memory phases and bounded producer queues; pin exact settings. Do not adopt global kernel tuning or a new RT kernel blindly; the repository already investigated some kernel work. An AXI hardware QoS regulator is a larger follow-on, justified only if measured contention dominates and a lighter mechanism fails. Do not change production behavior to latest-only dropping while continuing to claim every window was classified.

**Optional true-stream ingress test.** If the intended paper emphasizes lossless acquisition, build an isolated PL-paced input source or a carefully specified autonomous DMA replay wrapper, plus sequence counter and bounded FIFO/double buffering. Deliver the same exact raw values and full-window preprocessing format. Record sample/window acquisition-start/end, FIFO occupancy/high-watermark, overflow, accepted frame and result frame. Test whether incoming data is accepted while inference runs. Clearly call this laboratory replay, not a real sensor. An extra pair of 32,768-byte frame buffers is 64 KiB of storage before packing/overhead; fit and placement must be measured, even though baseline raw-core BRAM utilization suggests a pilot is plausible. Do not assume simple-mode DMA automatically becomes autonomous scatter/gather.

**Fair comparison.** Same overlay/model/input values, release period, transport, stressor rate, warm-up and temperature bounds. Separate quiet baseline, interference baseline and proposed isolation policy. If adding buffers, compare numerical identity and measure end-to-end age, not just transfer throughput. Never hide discarded or overwritten inputs. Added queueing can improve throughput while making diagnoses stale.

**Proposed gate.** For each declared operating point: zero malformed/gap/duplicate frames, zero wrong logits, zero sample/FIFO overflow, finite bounded queue occupancy, and zero release-to-result deadline misses over a predeclared 30,000-window scored run. For a mitigation claim, require a measured expansion of the admitted workload envelope at the same criteria, or a >=25% reduction of the worst measured response at a matched failing/tight operating point. Finite testing establishes an observed bound under those conditions, not a formal universal WCET guarantee. Stop increasing load after the declared failure criterion/temperature guard triggers; record the boundary.

**What must be captured with the board.** Trace all four timing points, source/queue occupancy (if implemented), exact stress rate/configuration, scheduling/affinity/kernel, DMA/register status, event counts, correctness hashes and temperatures. A PL-paced source requires its own bitstream, post-route report, counter specification and signal/timestamp validation. Plots and queue analyses can wait.

**Effort estimate.** 1–3 days for a reproducible no-build interference matrix; 3–7 additional days for a bounded-buffer hardware replay wrapper, uncertain until the current DMA/block design is inspected further. No special sensor equipment required. A GPIO/logic analyzer is optional for independently checking time alignment. If quiet and realistic loaded conditions already leave ample margin, do not turn this into an extensive memory-regulator project: preserve the envelope evidence and prioritize Candidates A/B.

### Gaps

- The exact implemented DDR/HP-port topology, DMA capabilities and spare BRAM of the final vote overlay require reading its HWH/block design before selecting a wrapper.
- Current release-to-result tests establish a prepared-input computation boundary; they do not establish true sensor acquisition timing, physical onset delay, or acquisition-to-screen timing.
- A universal “hard real-time under every workload” statement is unavailable. It would need a specified workload/admission model and stronger analysis than finite stress tests.

## 3. What was searched, what was rejected, and what should be preserved now?

### Takeaway

Choose one substantive new contribution (A first, B second) and capture its decisive physical evidence while the board exists. Keep C as the system validation envelope, expanding it only when it reveals a concrete engineering problem. Adaptive skipping, unsafe fault mechanisms, blanket TMR and grand sensor-generalization claims should not become default additions.

### Cited Findings

- FIRMBOUND ICLR 2025 learns finite-horizon stopping rules and sufficient statistics. It treats speed–accuracy as a Bayes-risk problem and explicitly discusses the importance of calibrated likelihood ratios. It includes non-i.i.d. experiments, so we must not imply that sequential methods universally require independent samples; rather, the specific likelihood model/guarantee must handle dependence. [FIRMBOUND author paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18059v1), introduction, definition 2.1, calibration discussion, appendix P.
- A related 2025 primary paper combines normalizing flows and SPRT for early time-series classification. Its snippets were screened, but direct DOI and ScienceDirect retrieval failed; do not rely on an unread full-paper detail. [Jo and Choi, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icte.2025.09.015).
- A 2024 FINN BNN study performs configuration-memory and exhaustive user-flip-flop fault campaigns. This is another reason to distinguish our possible logical-hook campaign from low-level physical configuration experiments. [Souvatzoglou et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01757). Abstract read; HTML full-text unavailable.
- The repository requires a class result for every prepared window and forbids hard-coded model bypass. Its current expected raw/window arithmetic and golden outputs are explicit. [Project instructions](../../../AGENTS.md), [HLS source contract](../../../hls/f1_int10_seq256/vitis/transformer_top_seq256_raw1024_dsp_margin_vote.cpp).

### Inferences

**Rejected or lower-priority additions.**

- Event-triggered inference skipping is an interesting independent design but conflicts with the current per-window result/no-bypass contract unless scope is explicitly changed. Reusing a previous class is not newly computed FPGA classification. A sentinel can flag a change or schedule collection without replacing the mandated classifier; that alone does not justify a new paper.
- Do not use DVFS glitches, voltage undervolting, overclocking, power-wasters, radiation, invasive probing or configuration-bit mutation as the first experiment. Deterministic internal logical fault hooks give a reviewable experiment without those mechanisms. Do not call the result radiation hardening.
- Blanket TMR/duplicate inference is a poor first implementation given 210 DSP use and narrow deadline slack. Selective exact checking is the interesting constrained question.
- Sliding-window overlap or a shorter window would change input/preprocessing and potentially retraining. It is a distinct variant requiring authorization, a new exported reference, training/validation selection and a correctly revised arrival period. It is not a free latency improvement.
- A sequence benchmark alone is not a new algorithm. Its value is as an open, reproducible test for startup, controlled transitions, short events, unresolved detections, corrupted state and deadline behavior, supporting A/B/C.

**Preserve before board return.** For each physical variant, archive source/config commit; bit/HWH/XSA plus hashes; exact runtime environment and command; unmodified approved golden data; input IDs and ordered replay manifest; C-model outputs; raw output logits/classes/state/flags; acquisition and result timing boundaries; route utilization/timing; guard/temperature log; injection site/bit/time manifest; failure/abort outputs as well as successes. Keep production and instrumented fault bitstreams distinct. Archive deterministic small replay packages that can later be audited without the board; handle MAFAULDA redistribution rights before publishing raw data.

### Gaps

- No claim of exhaustive literature coverage or first-ever novelty is justified by this bounded search.
- Effort estimates assume one researcher familiar with the current HLS and board toolchain, available machine time and no external equipment procurement. They are planning estimates, not a promised schedule.
- A real sensor rig, external labelled machine data and independent healthy operating hours would support stronger physical claims, but none was assumed available. They cannot be replaced by repeated board playback.
